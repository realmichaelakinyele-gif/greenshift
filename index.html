SYSTEM OVERVIEW

How biological treatment differs

Both approaches need the right capacity and final wastewater route. The main difference is how treatment takes place.

CONVENTIONAL SEPTIC

Settlement and storage

Solids settle in a tank while liquid moves to a soakaway or another locally approved discharge point. Inspection and desludging are part of ongoing care.

  • Simple tank-based separation
  • Requires a suitable outlet area
  • Service intervals vary with use
BIOLOGICAL TREATMENT

Staged biological breakdown

Microorganisms help break down organic matter in the treatment chambers. The system still needs suitable sizing, inspection and a site-appropriate discharge design.

  • Several treatment stages
  • Gravity flow where site levels allow
  • Maintenance depends on use and design

System performance and maintenance needs depend on the installation, occupancy and local requirements.

HOW IT WORKS

How the Biological Digestion Process Works

A gravity-fed process in which microorganisms break down organic matter before the liquid moves to a site-appropriate final treatment or dispersal stage.

01

Primary Inflow & Gravitational Separation

Blackwater enters the primary anaerobic chamber. Heavy organic solids settle to the lower digestion floor while scum and fats are baffled at the surface, isolating the aqueous middle layer.

Settling
02

Anaerobic Enzymatic Digestion

In an oxygen-limited chamber, microorganisms break down some of the organic material in wastewater over time. The tank needs suitable capacity and regular inspection.

Digestion
03

Multi-Tier Porous Bio-Filtration

Some systems use additional filter media to support further treatment and retain suspended material. The media and performance depend on the selected design.

Filter media
04

Clean Subsurface Dispersion & Reclamation

Treated liquid moves to a suitable final stage. Whether soil dispersal or further polishing is appropriate depends on local requirements and site conditions.

Final treatment

SOIL & SITE CONDITIONS

Custom Engineering for Problematic Soil Strata

Soil, groundwater and available space affect where a system can be installed and how treated water can be managed. A site assessment helps determine suitable options.

Black Cotton & Expansive Clay

Challenge: Zero natural water drainage; soil swells and cracks masonry.

Expansive clay can limit drainage and shift as moisture changes. A qualified designer should assess the ground and select a suitable tank foundation and wastewater dispersal method.

High Water Table & Coastal Regions

Challenge: Upward hydrostatic buoyancy causes traditional tanks to float or flood.

High groundwater can affect excavation, tank stability and the final discharge route. The design may need buoyancy and water ingress controls based on local ground conditions.

Hard Rock & Shallow Strata

Challenge: Inability to excavate deep soak pits without expensive blasting.

Shallow rock can limit excavation depth and affect cost. A site review can identify practical tank placement and an appropriate effluent management approach.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about installation, use and maintenance.

Anaerobic treatment can reduce the amount of solids that need removal, but no system is maintenance-free. Inspection and pumping needs depend on the design, occupancy and how the system is used. Follow the service guidance for your installation.

Use household cleaning products as directed. Avoid disposing of concentrated chemicals or large amounts of bleach into the system; they may disrupt biological treatment. Ask your installer for product-specific guidance.

Ask the installer whether a starter culture or later dosing is required for your model. Guidance depends on the product, operating conditions and service plan.

A retrofit may be possible after the tank's condition, capacity and effluent route have been assessed. The work can include repairs or changes to internal baffles and the final dispersal stage; some tanks may need replacement.

Flood performance depends on site levels, groundwater, installation details and maintenance. Keep surface water away from access covers and ask the designer how the system should be protected at your site.

Requirements vary by location and project. Confirm approvals, discharge conditions and warranty terms with the local authority and your installer before construction.
